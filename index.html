    1. 01 / The gap

      Most reports never meet each other.

      A photo in a neighborhood chat. A call to the ward office. A comment under a news post. Every one of them is real, and none of them knows the others exist.

    2. A location gives every report an address.

      Once a report is pinned, duplicates stand next to each other and a lone complaint stops being invisible. UrbanPulse asks for coordinates before it accepts anything.

    3. Pins add up to a pattern.

      A dense cluster shows where a street, a drain, or a light keeps failing. That is what a maintenance planner can act on, and what a resident can point to.

      SDG 9: Industry, Innovation and Infrastructure

      Field reports from residents tell crews where to look first, so repairs can follow evidence instead of guesswork.

      SDG 11: Sustainable Cities and Communities

      A shared, public record of problems and their status lets residents take part in how their neighborhood is maintained.

      05 / Who does what

      One report, four hand-offs.

      01 / 04

      01

      Resident

      Pin the spot

      Click the map and the coordinates are captured. A draggable pin lets you correct it before you send anything.

      02

      Platform

      Record it

      The form is validated, a report ID and timestamp are generated, and the first timeline entry is written. The marker appears on the map immediately.

      03

      Officer, simulated

      Update it

      Status changes go through the same shared store as new reports, so the map, the timeline, and the counts all move together. Officer mode in this build is a simulation.

      04

      Everyone

      Read the pattern

      Filter the map by category and status, rank reports by upvotes, and compare groups in the dataset. The pattern is the product.
